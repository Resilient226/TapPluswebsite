How DJs Can Share Booking Links and Music in Person

You meet a promoter after a set. Someone asks where they can hear your mixes. A venue manager wants your booking details. The connection is useful—but only if they can find the right information later.

For DJs, selectors, and independent performers · 5-minute read

For DJs, networking happens in motion: backstage, at events, between sets, and in quick conversations with people who may book you or share your work. The goal is not to hand over every link you own. It is to make the next step obvious.

1. Choose one main destination

Start by deciding where a new contact should land. That destination might be a portfolio page, a booking page, a link hub, or your own website. Keep the most important action easy to find.

  • For promoters and venues: make booking contact details easy to spot.
  • For listeners: prioritize mixes, releases, and the platform you update consistently.
  • For collaborators: show a short bio and the best way to reach you.

A single destination is easier to share and update than a long list of separate URLs. Review it periodically; broken or outdated links create friction.

2. Keep the information current

Before a major event or networking night, test your links on a phone. Check that the page loads quickly, the booking email is correct, and the music you want people to hear is accessible. Do not make someone search through old posts to find your current contact information.

3. Pick a sharing method that fits the moment

Link in your social bio

A useful default when someone already follows you online. It depends on the person finding your profile and opening the link.

QR code

Useful on flyers, posters, event materials, or a phone screen. People need to scan it with their camera or a compatible scanner.

NFC tap-to-share card

A physical card can open a linked digital destination when tapped with a compatible phone. It is useful when you are meeting someone face-to-face and want to make the handoff quickly.

None of these methods replaces good music, professionalism, or follow-through. Choose the one you will actually carry and keep updated.

4. Make the follow-up easy

After you meet someone, send a brief follow-up that connects to the conversation: a mix you discussed, a booking page, or a one-page press kit. A useful follow-up beats a generic “great meeting you” message with no next step.

Simple rule: one clear destination, current information, and a specific next step. More links do not automatically make a better introduction.
